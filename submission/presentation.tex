\begin{frame}[plain]
  \vfill
  {\color{accent}\LARGE\bfseries PSEL4 is a drop-in auto level for pytket-quantinuum\par}
  \vspace{0.4cm}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{3pt}\large
    \item PSEL4 is a cost-aware auto level. It compiles each circuit four ways and keeps the cheapest checked result.
    \item It is never costlier than the default compile (0 of 217 circuits).
    \item It needs 15.7\,\% less HQC than the default level 2 on our 217-circuit benchmark.
    \item \Otag\ Workload assumption: a team spends the Standard Plan's 10,000 HQC a month on this mix. PSEL4 then frees 1,573.3 HQC, worth USD 19,666 a month.

\begin{frame}{An H2 user pays HQC for every compile choice}
  \small
  \[ \mathrm{HQC} = 5 + C\,\bigl(N_{1q} + 10\,N_{2q} + 5\,N_{\mathrm{SPAM}}\bigr) / 5000 \]
  {\footnotesize\color{muted}$C$ is the shot count. $N_{1q}$ counts PhasedX gates, $N_{2q}$ counts ZZPhase and ZZMax gates and $N_{\mathrm{SPAM}}$ counts initialisations, measurements and resets. Rz gates are free.\par}
  \medskip
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{4pt}
    \item \textbf{User.} A team that runs circuits on H2 under a fixed HQC budget.
    \item pytket-quantinuum compiles at optimisation level 2 by default.
    \item Always using level 3 saves 13.9\,\% HQC, yet costs more than level 2 on 45 of 217 circuits.
    \item Blind ZX rewriting (PyZX teleport\_reduce) before level 2 costs 6.27\,\% more HQC on 180 Trotter circuits.
    \item \Otag\ The user cannot tell in advance which compile is cheapest for a given circuit.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 3
\begin{frame}{PSEL4 compiles four ways, checks each and keeps the lowest HQC}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{4pt}
        \item \textbf{Candidates.} L2 (default), L3, and PyZX teleport\_reduce followed by L2 (P2) or L3 (P2L3).
        \item \textbf{Check.} Each candidate is checked against its input as a full unitary up to 8 qubits and on 64 random computational basis inputs (including all-zero) from 9 to 20 qubits. Failed candidates are dropped.
        \item Above 20 qubits family C relies on PyZX miter proofs.
        \item \textbf{Guarantee.} L2 and L3 are always candidates, so PSEL4 never costs more than either where both pass the check.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.42\textwidth}
      \centering
      \begin{tikzpicture}[
          box/.style={draw=accent,rounded corners=2pt,fill=accent!6,minimum height=0.55cm,align=center,font=\footnotesize},
          arr/.style={-{Stealth[length=5pt]},color=accent!80}]
        \node[box,minimum width=3.0cm] (in) at (0,0) {input circuit};
        \node[box,minimum width=1.05cm] (p0) at (-1.95,-1.15) {L2};
        \node[box,minimum width=1.05cm] (l3) at (-0.65,-1.15) {L3};
        \node[box,minimum width=1.05cm] (p2) at (0.65,-1.15) {P2};
        \node[box,minimum width=1.05cm] (p2l3) at (1.95,-1.15) {P2L3};
        \node[box,minimum width=3.0cm] (chk) at (0,-2.35) {exactness check};
        \node[box,minimum width=3.0cm] (sel) at (0,-3.3) {keep lowest HQC};

        \draw[arr] (chk) -- (sel);
      \end{tikzpicture}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vspace{0.35cm}
  {\footnotesize\textbf{Drop-in use.} One command per circuit file, printing every candidate's HQC and the pick.\par}
  \smallskip

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Limitations}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{4pt}
    \item HQC is the formula estimate. All fidelity results come from the H2-2E emulator. Nothing has run on H2 hardware.
    \item On cdkm\_adder\_10 the level 3 pick returns the correct sum less often (0.8267 against 0.8692, $z = -2.90$). Accuracy-bound users need a fidelity-aware choice.
    \item The ZX-only gain is 0.26\,\% on the 17 proven family C circuits.
    \item Per correct answer: three of the six pairs compare P2, not the PSEL4 pick. Inputs are pooled as one binomial.
    \item 2 family C level 3 outputs and 3 PSEL4 outputs are unproven. On draper\_adder\_28 the PSEL4 pick starts from angles PyZX rounded by up to 1.50e-06 rad.
    \item The earlier vbe\_adder\_3 fidelity gain used the all-zero input. Its Holm-adjusted $p$ is 0.052 over three circuits.
    \item No user interview or survey is part of this submission. The selector follows pytket's PassSelector pattern.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 10
\begin{frame}{Finals plan is a fidelity-aware learned selector}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{5pt}
    \item \Otag\ Goal: a learned selector that predicts fidelity as well as HQC for each candidate. It keeps the per-answer saving and protects accuracy where users need it.
    \item cdkm\_adder\_10 shows why. Its cheaper compile costs less per correct sum but returns the correct sum less often.
    \item A first classifier on input-circuit features does not beat constant choices with a whole family held out (balanced accuracy 0.433 against 0.500).
    \item \Otag\ The finals model therefore needs structural features and emulator labels.
    \item \Otag\ Its starting data are the four-candidate ledger of 217 circuits and the 34 stored H2-2E jobs.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

    \item \textbf{Generated tables.} Value tables come from the scripts under \code{value/}. Emulator tables are regenerated offline from the stored payloads.
