\begin{frame}[plain]
  \vfill
  {\color{accent}\LARGE\bfseries PSEL4 is a drop-in auto level for pytket-quantinuum\par}
  \vspace{0.4cm}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{3pt}
    \item \textbf{Problem, measured.} pytket-quantinuum's level 3 saves 13.9\,\% HQC over the default level 2 on 217 circuits, but costs more on 45 of them (372.0 HQC lost, worst 12.95\,\% on gf2\_pow\_4\_mult). \Otag\ A user cannot tell in advance which.
    \item \textbf{PSEL4 removes that risk.} It compiles each circuit four ways (level 2, level 3, ZX before each), checks each result and keeps the cheapest. Costlier than level 2 on none of the 217.
    \item 15.7\,\% below level 2. 2.08\,\% (1,051.6 HQC) below always level 3. 1.35\,\% (679.6 HQC) below the per-circuit cheaper of level 2 and level 3.
    \item \Otag\ Workload assumption: a team spends the Standard Plan's 10,000 HQC a month on this mix. PSEL4 then frees 1,573.3 HQC, worth USD 19,666 a month.
  \end{itemize}

\begin{frame}{An H2 user pays HQC for every compile choice}
  \small
  \[ \mathrm{HQC} = 5 + C\,\bigl(N_{1q} + 10\,N_{2q} + 5\,N_{\mathrm{SPAM}}\bigr) / 5000 \]
  {\footnotesize\color{muted}$C$ is the shot count. $N_{1q}$ counts PhasedX gates, $N_{2q}$ counts ZZPhase and ZZMax gates and $N_{\mathrm{SPAM}}$ counts initialisations, measurements and resets. Rz gates are free.\par}
  \medskip
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{4pt}
    \item \textbf{User.} A team that runs circuits on H2 under a fixed HQC budget.
    \item pytket-quantinuum compiles at optimisation level 2 by default.
    \item Always using level 3 saves 13.9\,\% HQC, yet costs more than level 2 on 45 of 217 circuits.
    \item Blind ZX rewriting (PyZX teleport\_reduce) before level 2 costs 6.27\,\% more HQC on 180 Trotter circuits.
    \item \Otag\ The user cannot tell in advance which compile is cheapest for a given circuit.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 3
\begin{frame}{PSEL4 compiles four ways, checks each and keeps the lowest HQC}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{4pt}
        \item \textbf{Candidates.} L2 (default), L3, and PyZX teleport\_reduce followed by L2 (P2) or L3 (P2L3).
        \item \textbf{Check.} Each candidate is checked against its input as a full unitary up to 8 qubits and on 64 random computational basis inputs (including all-zero) from 9 to 20 qubits. Failed candidates are dropped.
        \item Above 20 qubits family C relies on PyZX miter proofs.
        \item \textbf{Guarantee.} L2 and L3 are always candidates, so PSEL4 never costs more than either where both pass the check.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.42\textwidth}
      \centering
      \begin{tikzpicture}[
          box/.style={draw=accent,rounded corners=2pt,fill=accent!6,minimum height=0.55cm,align=center,font=\footnotesize},
          arr/.style={-{Stealth[length=5pt]},color=accent!80}]

        \node[box,minimum width=1.05cm] (p2) at (0.65,-1.15) {P2};

      \end{tikzpicture}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vspace{0.35cm}
  {\footnotesize\textbf{Drop-in use.} One command per circuit file, printing every candidate's HQC and the pick.\par}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Value of the saving in USD}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{5pt}
    \item Azure plan averages are USD 10.29 (Premium) to 12.50 (Standard) per HQC.
    \item Against always level 2, PSEL4 saves 9,256.6 HQC, worth USD 95,289 to 115,707.
    \item Against always level 3, PSEL4 saves 1,051.6 HQC, worth USD 10,825 to 13,145.
    \item Always level 3 loses USD 3,829 to 4,650 on its 45 costlier circuits. PSEL4 loses nothing there.
    \item \Otag\ Workload assumption: a team spends the Standard Plan's 10,000 HQC a month on this circuit mix. PSEL4 then frees 1,573.3 HQC a month, worth USD 19,666 at the Standard average.
  \end{itemize}

\begin{frame}{Finding 3. Level 3 cuts two-qubit gates and ZX cuts phases}
  \centering\small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrrrrr@{}}
    \toprule
    & \multicolumn{4}{c}{Non-Clifford gates} & \multicolumn{4}{c}{Two-qubit gates} \\
    \cmidrule(lr){2-5}\cmidrule(l){6-9}
    Set & L2 & P2 & L3 & P2L3 & L2 & P2 & L3 & P2L3 \\
    \midrule
    B1, 14 circuits & 877 & 656 & 746 & 701 & 862 & 799 & 723 & 700 \\
    C, 15 circuits & 3323 & 2930 & 2540 & 2535 & 3208 & 2944 & 2718 & 2708 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \src{Totals after each pipeline. 6 circuits did not fit the compile budget. Source \code{value/mechanism\_zx\_l3.md}.}
  \raggedright\footnotesize
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
    \item \textbf{Mechanism.} HQC charges no Rz gate and bills every PhasedX at the same rate, so the T-count reduction ZX is best at has no HQC value on H2, and ZX can pay mainly through two-qubit gates, which level 3 already resynthesises.
    \item GreedyPauliSimp in level 3 lowers the two-qubit count on 180 of 180 A, 13 of 14 B1 and 9 of 15 C circuits.
    \item teleport\_reduce leaves fewer non-Clifford gates on B1 (656 against 746 for level 3). A two-qubit gate costs 2 HQC at 1000 shots and a PhasedX 0.2.
    \item ZX on top of the cheaper pytket level saves 3.21\,\% on all 20 family C circuits (0.26\,\% on the 17 proven), 0.49\,\% on B1 and 0 on Trotter circuits. Its gain anticorrelates with the level 3 gain, $\rho = -0.64$.
    \item On B1 the ZX stage removes 40.6\,\% of T gates. The Gosset et al.\ (2014) bound leaves at most 0.3\,\% for further exact T optimisation (24 circuits of at most 5 qubits).
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Limitations}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{3pt}
    \item HQC is the formula estimate. All fidelity results come from the H2-2E emulator. Nothing has run on H2 hardware.
    \item The PSEL4 pick was significantly less accurate on 2 of 5 pre-registered pairs (cdkm\_adder\_10 $z = -2.90$, hwb6 $z = -2.64$). Accuracy-bound users need a fidelity-aware choice.
    \item Per-answer intervals pool inputs and ignore between-run variation. 3 of 8 per-answer pairs use P2, the earlier selector's pick.
    \item The ZX-only gain is 0.26\,\% on the 17 proven family C circuits.
    \item 2 family C level 3 outputs and 3 PSEL4 outputs are unproven. On draper\_adder\_28 the PSEL4 pick starts from angles PyZX rounded by up to 1.50e-06 rad.
    \item The earlier vbe\_adder\_3 fidelity gain used the all-zero input. Its Holm-adjusted $p$ is 0.052 over three circuits.
    \item No user interview or survey is part of this submission. The selector follows pytket's PassSelector pattern.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 10
\begin{frame}{Finals plan is a fidelity-aware learned selector}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{5pt}
    \item \Otag\ Goal: a learned selector that predicts fidelity as well as HQC for each candidate. It keeps the per-answer saving and protects accuracy where users need it.
    \item Finding 2 shows why. On 2 of 5 pre-registered pairs the HQC-cheapest compile returns the correct output less often.
    \item Submission 1 provides the training data: the checked ledger (217 circuits, 4 candidates each) and the emulator labels (5 pre-registered PSEL4-pick pairs, 48 H2-2E jobs).
    \item A first classifier on input-circuit features does not beat a constant choice with a whole family held out (balanced accuracy 0.433 against 0.500).
    \item \Otag\ The model therefore needs compiled-candidate structural features and emulator labels.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
