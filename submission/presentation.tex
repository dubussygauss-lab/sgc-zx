\begin{frame}{Blind ZX rewriting can raise the cost of an H2 run}
  \small
  A run on Quantinuum H2 is billed in HQC.
  \[ \mathrm{HQC} = 5 + C\,\bigl(N_{1q} + 10\,N_{2q} + 5\,N_{\mathrm{SPAM}}\bigr) / 5000 \]
  {\footnotesize\color{muted} $C$ is the shot count. $N_{1q}$ counts PhasedX gates. $N_{2q}$ counts ZZPhase and ZZMax gates. $N_{\mathrm{SPAM}}$ counts initialisations, measurements and resets. Rz gates are free.\par}
  \smallskip
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
    \item ZX rewriting (PyZX teleport\_reduce) placed before pytket's Quantinuum pass can lower this cost or raise it.
    \item Run blindly before level 2, it costs 6.27\,\% more HQC on 180 Trotter circuits and 22.1\,\% more on 3 QFT adders.
    \item On draper\_adder\_16 it costs 1,385.0 HQC against 906.8 for level 2.
    \item pytket's own \code{ZXGraphlikeOptimisation} followed by level 2 costs 59.22\,\% more than level 2 on 14 B1 circuits. Its documentation warns that it ``may increase the cost of the circuit''.
    \item Qiskit at optimisation level 3, rebased to H2, also costs more than level 2.
    \item A team with a fixed HQC budget cannot tell in advance which circuits gain. \Otag
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 3
\begin{frame}{PSEL4 answers one question per circuit for a team on a fixed HQC budget}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{4pt}
    \item \textbf{User.} A team that runs arithmetic and oracle subroutines on Quantinuum H2 from a fixed HQC budget. \Otag
    \item \textbf{Decision.} Should this circuit go through ZX rewriting before pytket's Quantinuum pass, and at which optimisation level?
    \item \textbf{Answer.} PSEL4 compiles the circuit four ways and keeps the cheapest result that passes its checks. Level 2 and level 3 are always candidates.
    \item \textbf{Why per circuit.} On the 60 XXZ circuits teleport\_reduce before level 2 raises the total HQC by 12.33\,\%. On other circuits it lowers the cost. The choice depends on the circuit.
    \item \textbf{What the user gains.} The user tries ZX rewriting on every circuit for the price of extra compilations and never pays more HQC than level 2 or level 3. \Otag
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{PSEL4 keeps the lowest HQC of four pytket compilations}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
        \item \textbf{P0.} pytket-quantinuum 0.59.3 at optimisation level 2, pytket's default.
        \item \textbf{L3.} The same pass at optimisation level 3.
        \item \textbf{P2 and P2L3.} PyZX teleport\_reduce, then level 2 or level 3.
        \item A candidate that fails its exactness check or times out is dropped. Ties go to the lower two-qubit count.
        \item \textbf{Guarantee.} Level 2 and level 3 are always candidates. PSEL4 therefore never costs more HQC than either on a circuit where both pass its checks. This holds by construction.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}

\begin{frame}{Outputs are checked exactly up to 20 qubits and proven on 17 of 20 larger circuits}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{4pt}
    \item \textbf{Exact checks.} PSEL4 checks every candidate up to 20 qubits. On families A, B1 and B2 every compiled circuit is checked against the uncompiled reference with tolerance 1e-8. All 1260 family A rows, 105 B1 rows and 100 B2 rows pass.
    \item \textbf{Miter proofs.} A PyZX miter reduction proves the PSEL4 output equal to its input as a full unitary up to global phase on 17 of the 20 family C circuits of 20 to 56 qubits.
    \item \textbf{Not proven.} hrs\_mult\_5 has off-grid angles and passes a check on 64 random basis inputs only. The miters of rgqft\_mult\_6 and draper\_adder\_28 timed out after 300 s.
    \item \textbf{Official pass.} Our level 2 pipelines give the same counts as the official pytket-quantinuum pass on all 445 compiled rows of families A, B1, B1x and B2. Family C was not compiled with the official pass.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A known T-count bound shows how much exact T optimisation can still gain}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{3pt}
    \item Each T gate of a surface-code machine consumes one magic state. On H2 a T gate is a free Rz.
    \item On B1 the ZX stage removes 2,862 of 7,048 T gates of the written circuits, or 40.6\,\%.
    \item The floor is the known channel bound of Gosset et al.\ (2014), evaluated exactly in integer arithmetic. On 20 synthesised Haar circuits it is 0.997 of the T-count after ZX, pooled.
    \item Further exact T optimisation can gain at most 0.3\,\% pooled and 0.72\,\% on the worst instance.
    \item The evaluation covers 24 circuits of at most 5 qubits. It covers no circuit of family C.
  \end{itemize}
  \assess{The bound itself is not new. Its exact evaluation tells a fault-tolerant team where further T optimisation does not pay.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Limitations}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
    \item No user interview or survey is part of this submission.
    \item The ZX-only gain is small. On the 17 proven family C circuits it is 0.26\,\% on top of the cheaper pytket level.
    \item 3 family C picks are unproven. 87.6\,\% of the family C saving against level 3 comes from 2 of them, hrs\_mult\_5 and draper\_adder\_28.
    \item The vbe\_adder\_3 fidelity gain has a pooled Holm-adjusted $p$ of 0.052 over three circuits. The repeats-only check gives 0.030 and was chosen after the pooled result.
    \item The earlier emulator runs of vbe\_adder\_3 and grover\_5 used the all-zero input.
    \item HQC is the formula estimate. Nothing has run on H2 hardware.
    \item The T-count floor is the known Gosset et al.\ bound, evaluated on 24 circuits of at most 5 qubits.
    \item The selector follows pytket's PassSelector pattern.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
