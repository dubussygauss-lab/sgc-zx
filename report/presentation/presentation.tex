\begin{frame}{ZX rewriting before pytket can raise the cost of an H2 run}
  \small
  A run on Quantinuum H2 is billed in HQC.
  \[ \mathrm{HQC} = 5 + C\,\bigl(N_{1q} + 10\,N_{2q} + 5\,N_{\mathrm{SPAM}}\bigr) / 5000 \]
  {\footnotesize\color{muted} $C$ is the shot count. $N_{1q}$ counts PhasedX gates. $N_{2q}$ counts ZZPhase and ZZMax gates. $N_{\mathrm{SPAM}}$ counts initialisations, measurements and resets. Rz gates are free.\par}
  \medskip
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{3pt}
    \item ZX rewriting (PyZX teleport\_reduce) placed before pytket's Quantinuum pass can lower this cost or raise it.
    \item Always run, it costs 6.27\,\% more HQC than pytket alone on 180 Trotter circuits and 22.1\,\% more on 3 QFT adders.
    \item On draper\_adder\_16 it costs 1,385.0 HQC against 906.8.
    \item pytket's own ZX pass \code{ZXGraphlikeOptimisation} is documented as one that ``may increase the cost of the circuit''.
    \item A team with a fixed HQC budget cannot tell in advance which circuits gain. \Otag
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 3
\begin{frame}{PSEL answers one question per circuit for a team on a fixed HQC budget}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{4pt}
    \item \textbf{User.} A team that runs arithmetic and oracle subroutines on Quantinuum H2 from a fixed HQC budget. \Otag
    \item \textbf{Decision.} Should this circuit go through ZX rewriting before pytket's Quantinuum pass?
    \item \textbf{Answer.} PSEL compiles the circuit both ways and keeps the cheaper result that passes its checks. P0 is always a candidate. PSEL therefore never costs more HQC than P0 when P0 passes its checks.
    \item \textbf{Why per circuit.} On the 60 XXZ circuits P2 alone raises the total HQC by 12.33\,\%. PSEL keeps P0 on all 60.
    \item \textbf{Finals roadmap.} Learned ZX rewriting is the finals goal. A model will predict which ZX rewrites lower HQC. The P0 and P2 rows of the PSEL ledger are its training data. \Otag
  \end{itemize}
  \medskip
  {\footnotesize\color{muted}No user interview or survey is part of this submission.\par}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{PSEL compiles each circuit twice and keeps the cheaper result}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.60\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
        \item \textbf{P0.} pytket-quantinuum 0.59.3 default pass at optimisation level 2. The baseline.
        \item \textbf{P2.} PyZX teleport\_reduce, then P0.
        \item \textbf{PSEL} drops a candidate that fails its exactness check and keeps the one with the lower HQC. Ties go to the lower two-qubit count, then to P0.
        \item \textbf{Official baseline.} Our P0 and P2 give the same counts as the official pytket-quantinuum pass on all 445 compiled rows of families A, B1, B1x and B2. Family C was not compiled with the official pass.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.37\textwidth}

\begin{frame}{The selector avoids the losses of always running ZX}
  \small
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      {\color{accent}\textbf{Guard against always running P2}}
      \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
        \item 180 Trotter circuits. P2 alone would cost 1,030.4 HQC or 6.27\,\% more than P0.
        \item PSEL avoids 1,076.4 HQC there against always running P2. It still saves 46.0 HQC against P0.
        \item 3 QFT adders. P2 is dearer on all three. PSEL keeps P0 and avoids 1,057.2 HQC.
        \item PSEL never raises the two-qubit count against P0 on the 223 circuits of families A, B1, B1x and B2.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      {\color{accent}\textbf{Clifford+T benchmarks B1}}
      \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
        \item 15 circuits with $n \le 12$. PSEL picks P2 on 14.
        \item PSEL saves 743.6 of 15,516.0 HQC or 4.79\,\%.
        \item The median saving per circuit is 3.94\,\%.
        \item hwb8 alone gives 84.5\,\% of the saving and serves no sourced quantum algorithm. Without hwb8 the saving is 115.0 HQC.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \assess{The median is the better guide for a single small circuit.}

\begin{frame}{A certified floor bounds what exact T optimisation can still gain}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{3pt}
    \item Each T gate of a surface-code machine consumes one magic state. On H2 a T gate is a free Rz.
    \item On B1 the ZX stage removes 2,862 of 7,048 T gates of the written circuits, or 40.6\,\%.
    \item The floor is the channel bound of Gosset et al.\ (2014). On 20 synthesised Haar circuits of 2 and 3 qubits it is 0.997 of the T-count after ZX, pooled. The worst instance keeps at least 2478 of its 2496 T gates.
    \item Further exact T optimisation can gain at most 0.3\,\% pooled and 0.72\,\% on the worst instance.
    \item On the four certified B1 circuits the six published optimisers close 1 of the 59 gates between our count and the floor.
    \item The certificate covers 24 circuits with at most 5 qubits. It covers no circuit of family C and no Grover instance.
  \end{itemize}
  \assess{The certificate cannot tell whether the other 58 gates are headroom for a better rewrite or slack in the bound.}
\end{frame}
