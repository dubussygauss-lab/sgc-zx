[F] With a whole family held out, neither model beats the constant choices: pooled balanced accuracy is 0.433 for logistic regression and 0.398 for boosted trees against 0.500 for always P0 and always P2, and no model has lower regret than the better constant choice on any held-out family. [F] Under grouped 5-fold cross-validation over 38 construction groups, boosted trees reach a pooled regret of 1237.8 HQC against 2135.6 for always P2 and 5162.4 for always P0, but within each family their regret is never below the better constant choice. [O] The pooled gain therefore comes from telling family types apart from gate statistics, not from predicting which circuit inside a family benefits from ZX. [F] As a gate, the leave-one-family-out logistic regression skips 123 of 245 second compilations at a cost of 46.6 HQC (0.081 % of the oracle total), against an expected 2591.7 HQC for a random gate that skips as many. [F] On family A alone that gate costs 37.0 HQC against 27.6 HQC expected for a random gate with the same 108 skips. [O] The gate saves compile time cheaply because it skips circuits where the possible saving is small, and the two models disagree strongly, so this is a small honest learned component and not evidence that the benefit of ZX can be predicted.
