# Application value: who uses the circuits our results speak to

Every statement carries a tag. `[F]` means the statement was checked on 2026-10-09 against the cited source (arXiv abstract from the arXiv API, arXiv full-text PDF converted with pdftotext, or a web page downloaded that day) or recomputed from the cited repository file. `[O]` means an editorial judgement. Quotations are copied from the fetched text. In PDF quotations line breaks were joined and hyphenation split by pdftotext was rejoined. Anything that could not be fetched is marked Unverifiable.

Companion file: `docs/applications.md` maps the B1 and B1x circuits to algorithms. This file covers the application users behind families A, B2 and C.

## 1. Quantum finance: amplitude estimation needs quantum arithmetic

- `[F]` Stamatopoulos et al., "Option Pricing using Quantum Computers", [arXiv:1905.02666](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02666). Abstract: "We present a methodology to price options and portfolios of options on a gate-based quantum computer using amplitude estimation, an algorithm which provides a quadratic speedup compared to classical Monte Carlo methods." Full text: "The addition of the values in two quantum registers |a, b⟩ → |a, a + b⟩ may be calculated in quantum computers with adder circuits based on CNOT and Toffoli gates [38–40]." Full text on the payoff: "This operation can be implemented by a quantum comparator [36] based on CNOT and Toffoli gates."
- `[F]` Chakrabarti et al., "A Threshold for Quantum Advantage in Derivative Pricing", [arXiv:2012.03819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03819). Abstract: "We find that the benchmark use cases we examine require 8k logical qubits and a T-depth of 54 million." Full text, Section 1: "In this table, we quantify resource requirements in the fault tolerant setting where the number of T-gates, called the T-count dominates the computational requirements." Full text, Appendix C: "Quantum arithmetic is required for path loading using the Riemann summation method (Section 4.1) and the re-parameterization method (Section 4.2), as well as the payoff calculation described in Section 5." Appendix C costs addition, multiplication, square root and comparators in Toffoli gates, for example "Using the algorithm described in [34], we can perform addition of two n-qubit registers in place with a Toffoli cost of 10n − 3w(n) − ...".
- `[F]` Table 1 of arXiv:2012.03819 gives T-counts of 1.2 × 10^10 (autocallable) and 9.8 × 10^9 (TARF) for the re-parameterization method.
- `[F]` Woerner and Egger, "Quantum Risk Analysis", [arXiv:1806.06893](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.06893). Abstract: "We employ quantum amplitude estimation to evaluate risk measures such as Value at Risk and Conditional Value at Risk on a gate-based quantum computer." Full text: "With (n + 1) ancillas, adder-circuits can be used to construct Fl using O(n) gates [21], and the resulting convergence rate is O(M^−1)."
- Unverifiable: the brief's wording that arithmetic "dominates" the resources of derivative pricing. None of the three fetched texts states that arithmetic is the dominant share. arXiv:2012.03819 says the T-count dominates and that arithmetic is required in path loading and payoff, but it does not split the total T-count by arithmetic versus other components in the passages read. The paper also uses pre-trained variational circuits for path loading, so a part of the cost is rotations.
- `[F]` Liu et al., "Certified randomness using a trapped-ion quantum processor", [arXiv:2503.20498](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20498). Affiliations on page 1 include "Global Technology Applied Research, JPMorganChase" and "Quantinuum, Broomfield, CO". Abstract: "we demonstrate the generation of certifiably random bits using the 56-qubit Quantinuum H2-1 trapped-ion quantum computer accessed over the internet." This is a randomness protocol, not option pricing or portfolio work.
- Unverifiable: the Nature publication of the certified-randomness work. nature.com and jpmorganchase.com returned HTTP 403 through the proxy.
- Unverifiable: any Quantinuum and JPMorganChase option-pricing or portfolio paper on H-series hardware. None was fetched.

## 2. Chemistry and phase estimation: rotation synthesis sets the T budget

- `[F]` Ross and Selinger, "Optimal ancilla-free Clifford+T approximation of z-rotations", [arXiv:1403.2975](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.2975). Abstract: "In the typical case, this yields circuit approximations of T-count 3log_2(1/epsilon) + O(log(log(1/epsilon)))."
- `[F]` Selinger, "Efficient Clifford+T approximation of single-qubit operators", [arXiv:1212.6253](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.6253). Abstract: "If the operator to be approximated is a z-rotation, the resulting gate sequence has T-count K+4log_2(1/ε), where K is approximately equal to 10."
- `[F]` Kliuchnikov, Maslov, Mosca, "Fast and efficient exact synthesis of single qubit unitaries generated by Clifford and T gates", [arXiv:1206.5236](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5236). Abstract: "We report an efficient synthesis algorithm, with an exact optimality guarantee on the number of Hadamard and T gates used."
- `[F]` Reiher et al., "Elucidating Reaction Mechanisms on Quantum Computers" (FeMoco), [arXiv:1605.03590](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.03590). Full text: "Well-known methods exist for implementing each e^{−iH_j t/2r} arising in a second-quantized formulation using a sequence of elementary gates and single-qubit rotations [11]. These rotations dominate the cost of the quantum simulation." Full text: "The single-qubit rotations, however, require approximation by a discrete set of gates consisting of Clifford operations and at least one non-Clifford operation, usually taken to be the T gate, a rotation by π/8 about the z-axis." Abstract: "even when taking into account the substantial overhead of quantum error correction, and the need to compile into discrete gate sets, the necessary computations can be performed in reasonable time on small quantum computers."
- `[F]` Lee et al., "Even more efficient quantum computations of chemistry through tensor hypercontraction", [arXiv:2011.03494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03494). Abstract: "With O(λ/ε) repetitions of these circuits one can use phase estimation to sample in the molecular eigenbasis". Full text: "As these rotations add significant cost to the method, we also introduce a strategy for coarse graining the angles of the basis transformation". `[O]` In this qubitization approach the rotations are applied through a phase-gradient state and costed in Toffoli gates, not through gridsynth sequences. It supports the claim that rotations are a major cost, not the claim that gridsynth output is what gets compiled.
- `[F]` Beverland et al., "Assessing requirements to scale to practical quantum advantage", [arXiv:2211.07629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07629). Full text: "NonClifford rotations Rz(θ) are frequently used in quantum algorithms, but are among the most resource intensive operations when we consider implementation costs." Full text: "the QIR instruction set contains fine-angle rotation unitaries, which must be approximated with sufficient accuracy by sequences of operations from the planar quantum ISA, a process known as synthesis." Abstract: "we assess three scaled quantum applications and find that hundreds of thousands to millions of physical qubits are needed to achieve practical quantum advantage."
- `[F]` arXiv:2012.03819 (finance) Appendix C also covers rotation synthesis: steps 3 and 5 of its payoff "require controlled-Ry rotations whose decomposition into T-gates is a function of an additive error".

## 4. pytket users can apply ZX resynthesis themselves

- `[F]` pytket 2.18.5 API documentation, [docs.quantinuum.com/tket/api-docs/passes.html](https://docs.quantinuum.com/tket/api-docs/passes.html), entry `ZXGraphlikeOptimisation(allow_swaps: bool = True)`: "Attempt to optimise the circuit by simplifying in ZX calculus and extracting a circuit back out." Same entry: "As a resynthesis pass, this will ignore almost all optimisations achieved beforehand and may increase the cost of the circuit."
- `[F]` The same page documents `PassSelector(passlist, score_func)`: "Collection of pytket compilation passes which are all applied to the same circuit. The result of the compilation is the best circuit as selected by a given metric."
- `[F]` [docs.quantinuum.com/tket/api-docs/zx.html](https://docs.quantinuum.com/tket/api-docs/zx.html) documents the `pytket.zx` module with `ZXDiagram` and a list of `Rewrite` objects.
- Unverifiable: the pytket-pyzx extension. Its GitHub and PyPI pages returned HTTP 403 and the docs.quantinuum.com extension URLs tried returned 404. The extension list on the pytket 2.18.5 docs page does not include pytket-pyzx `[F]`.

## How our results map to these users

All numbers below were recomputed on 2026-10-09 from the cited files with Python and agree with the stored values.

- `[O]` Finance users. Amplitude-estimation pricing and risk circuits call adders, comparators and multipliers (Section 1). Family C contains these arithmetic blocks at 20 to 56 qubits. `[F]` On the 9 adder circuits PSEL lowers HQC from 5,777.0 to 5,031.4, a saving of 745.6 HQC or 12.9 % (`value/family_C_selector.md`, `value/family_C_selector.csv`). `[F]` On the 15 family C circuits without continuous angles PSEL lowers HQC from 10,019.6 to 9,271.2, a saving of 748.4 HQC or 7.47 % with 12 P2 picks (same files). `[O]` The 7.47 % subset is the fairer figure for a finance user because it excludes the two multipliers, which supply 3,356.0 of the 4,104.4 HQC saved over all 20 family C circuits (`value/family_C_selector.md`). `[O]` These are NISQ-era HQC savings at 1,000 shots and do not speak to the fault-tolerant T-depth that arXiv:2012.03819 estimates.
- `[O]` Chemistry and phase-estimation users. Reiher et al. and Beverland et al. name single-qubit rotations as a leading cost, and gridsynth turns each rotation into a Clifford+T sequence (Section 2). Family B2 consists of synthesized Clifford+T circuits of this kind. `[F]` Over the 20 B2 circuits the written T-count is 42,396, the ZX stage of P2 leaves 41,534 and the certified floor is 41,414 (`results/certificates.md`). `[F]` ZX removes 862 T gates, which is 2.0 % of the written T-count, and the floor covers 99.7 % of the T gates that remain (`results/certificates.md`, `value/value_headlines.csv`). `[O]` For a user whose T budget comes from rotation synthesis, ZX rewriting after synthesis returns 2.0 % and the P2 output sits only 120 T gates above the certified floor. The saving has to come from better synthesis, not from ZX post-processing.
- `[O]` pytket users who add ZX passes. The pytket documentation warns that `ZXGraphlikeOptimisation` "may increase the cost of the circuit" and pytket ships a `PassSelector` (Section 4). `[F]` On the 180 Trotter circuits of family A, always running P2 costs 17,453.0 HQC against 16,422.6 for P0, which is 6.27 % more (`value/selector_ledger.md`, `value/selector_ledger_circuits.csv`, `results/official_baseline.md`). `[F]` PSEL costs 16,376.6 HQC and avoids 1,076.4 HQC relative to always-P2 (same files). `[O]` A user who applies ZX without a selector pays this loss. PSEL is the cost-aware version of what `PassSelector` already allows, with HQC as the score function.
